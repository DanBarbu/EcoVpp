The basics

Do I need to install anything in my apartment?

No. You keep your existing electricity meter and everything else stays in place. All the work happens on the roof and in the building's electrical room.

Do I need to change my electricity supplier?

Usually not. In most countries you can join the community while keeping your current supplier, and the solar shared with you appears as a discount on your regular bill.

Can I leave the community if I move out or change my mind?

Yes. Signing up is voluntary and cancelling takes one form. The system re-shares your portion among the remaining members.

Is this really legal?

Yes — since 2018, the EU's Renewable Energy Directive explicitly enables residential energy communities. National implementations vary, and EcoVPP takes care of the local paperwork.

Money & savings

How much can I actually save?

Realistic savings on a typical apartment run around 15–40% of the electricity bill, depending on how much solar the building has, how sunny your area is, and how well your consumption matches production hours.

Who pays for the solar installation?

Options range from the building fund paying up-front to a partner installer financing it and taking a share of the savings for a few years. EcoVPP helps evaluate all three routes.

What happens to extra solar the building doesn't use?

Excess energy is sold back to the grid and the revenue goes to the community fund, usually credited to residents alongside their shared solar.

Privacy & data

Do you track my consumption?

Only the totals needed to calculate your fair share. That data stays inside the building's system by default. Anything that leaves it is opt-in.

Where is my data stored?

On EU-hosted infrastructure by default; buildings that want fully on-site hosting can run the system locally.

Do you sell any data?

No, never. EcoVPP is paid for by the building — not by anyone's personal data.

Practical

What if the roof isn't big enough for everyone?

Even a partial rooftop still saves money — every kWh shared is one less kWh paid to the utility. EcoVPP works whether the roof covers 20% or 100% of the building's needs.

What about apartments on the top floor with a private roof?

They can still join and benefit from the shared system, and often get slightly larger shares if they contribute their portion of the roof.

Can we add a battery later?

Yes. The platform is designed so you can start with solar-only and add storage or EV charging when it makes sense financially.
